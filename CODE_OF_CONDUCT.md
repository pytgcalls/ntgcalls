# Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make participation in our
community a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body
size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender
identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity
and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming,
diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Our Standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment for our
community include:

* Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people
* Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences
* Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback
* Accepting responsibility and apologizing to those affected by our mistakes,
  and learning from the experience
* Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but for the
  overall community

Examples of unacceptable behavior include:

* The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or
  advances of any kind
* Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
* Public or private harassment
* Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email
  address, without their explicit permission
* Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting

## Contribution Standards

These rules apply to every issue, pull request, review and discussion in this
repository. They are part of this Code of Conduct, and the enforcement section
below applies to them.

### Use of AI Tools

AI tools are welcome as assistants. They are not welcome as authors.

Pull requests whose code or description was produced by an AI tool and submitted
as it came out are not accepted, and are closed without review. The same goes
for issues that consist of a model's guesses about our code.

You may use AI tools to:

* explore the codebase, look for the cause of a bug, or make sense of an error;
* get suggestions while you write code, the way you would use autocompletion;
* draft tests, or review a patch you already wrote;
* help you write in English when it is not your first language.

In all of these cases you remain the author, which means:

* you understand every line you are submitting and can explain why it is there;
* you built the project and tested the change yourself, on a real call when the
  change touches calls;
* you take responsibility for the result, including what the tool got wrong.

**Disclosure is required.** If an AI tool contributed to a pull request, an
issue or a review, say so in the description: which tool, and what you used it
for. One line is enough, for example "the tests were drafted with an assistant,
I reviewed and ran them". Hiding AI involvement wastes maintainer time and is
treated as a violation of this Code of Conduct.

This applies to bug reports as well. An analysis generated by a model reads as
confidently when it is wrong as when it is right, and checking it costs more
than writing it. Report what you actually observed, attach logs, and mark
clearly the parts you have not verified yourself.

### Before Opening a Pull Request

Small self-contained fixes, such as a typo, a wrong constant or a one-line
correction with an obvious cause, can go straight to a pull request.

For anything larger, open an issue first and agree on the approach with the
maintainer. "Larger" includes:

* the public API of any binding, the schema, or the code generator;
* signaling, media handling, end-to-end encryption, or the WebRTC integration;
* new dependencies, or updates to existing ones;
* the build system, the CI workflows, or the release process;
* refactors, renames and reformatting, or anything touching many files;
* new features of any size.

A pull request that arrives without that conversation may be closed even when
the code is correct, because it can collide with work already in progress or
with a design decision that is not visible from the outside. Asking first costs
you a message and saves you a rewrite.

### Code Quality and Style

* Match the code around you. Naming, layout and structure should make your
  change indistinguishable from the file it lands in.
* The codebase carries almost no comments on purpose. Keep it that way: name
  things so the code explains itself, rather than explaining it afterwards.
* Prefer a private static method over a nested lambda when a helper grows past
  a couple of lines.
* One logical change per pull request. No drive-by reformatting, renaming or
  cleanups of unrelated code: they make the change harder to review and the
  history harder to read.
* Commit messages are written in Title Case and say what the change does,
  referencing the issue when there is one, for example
  `Fixed Crash When Removing An Incoming Video Channel (#69)`.
* Do not commit generated files, local build scripts or editor configuration.
* Say which platforms you built and tested on. A change that cannot be built on
  the platforms it affects cannot be merged.
* Bring evidence. What you ran, what happened before and what happens now. For
  crashes, a symbolized stack trace. For call problems, native logs with the
  `ntgcalls` and `webrtc` loggers at debug level, with credentials, tokens and
  addresses masked.

### Reviews and Merging

Only maintainers merge. Reviews can take time, sometimes a long time, and
silence is not rejection. Keep the discussion in the pull request or issue it
belongs to, so that whoever reads it later finds the reasoning next to the code.

Security issues are the exception to all of the above: do not open a public
issue or pull request for them, follow SECURITY.md instead.

## Enforcement Responsibilities

Community leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of
acceptable behavior and will take appropriate and fair corrective action in
response to any behavior that they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive,
or harmful.

Community leaders have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or reject
comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and other contributions that are
not aligned to this Code of Conduct, and will communicate reasons for moderation
decisions when appropriate.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all community spaces, and also applies when
an individual is officially representing the community in public spaces.
Examples of representing our community include using an official e-mail address,
posting via an official social media account, or acting as an appointed
representative at an online or offline event.

## Enforcement

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behavior may be
reported to the community leaders responsible for enforcement at
iraci.lauren@gmail.com.
All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly.

All community leaders are obligated to respect the privacy and security of the
reporter of any incident.

## Enforcement Guidelines

Community leaders will follow these Community Impact Guidelines in determining
the consequences for any action they deem in violation of this Code of Conduct:

### 1. Correction

**Community Impact**: Use of inappropriate language or other behavior deemed
unprofessional or unwelcome in the community.

**Consequence**: A private, written warning from community leaders, providing
clarity around the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the
behavior was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

### 2. Warning

**Community Impact**: A violation through a single incident or series
of actions.

**Consequence**: A warning with consequences for continued behavior. No
interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with
those enforcing the Code of Conduct, for a specified period of time. This
includes avoiding interactions in community spaces as well as external channels
like social media. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or
permanent ban.

### 3. Temporary Ban

**Community Impact**: A serious violation of community standards, including
sustained inappropriate behavior.

**Consequence**: A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public
communication with the community for a specified period of time. No public or
private interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction
with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, is allowed during this period.
Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban.

### 4. Permanent Ban

**Community Impact**: Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community
standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior,  harassment of an
individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence**: A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within
the community.
